01 / THE ROOM

Fifteen projectors, where most theaters use two or three.

Most projection theaters put two or three projectors on one screen. This room, built by Houston studio SIXTYFOUR, uses 15 Epson EB-L695SE laser projectors to cover three walls and the floor of a space of about 500 square feet.

That is 90,000 lumens of rated output and more than 30 million pixels in a room the size of a two-car garage.

02 / THE WALLS

Dark walls, on purpose.

The walls are not white. They are painted dark, with a light reflectance value (LRV) of about 11, so they reflect only about 11% of the light that hits them.

When every wall is a screen, light from one wall bounces onto the others and washes them out. Dark paint stops that, so blacks stay black and colors stay strong.

The cost is that the paint absorbs most of the projector light too. That is why the room needs so many projectors, so close together.

03 / THE PLAN

The whole rig, in FieldLux first.

A room like this is hard to plan by hand. Neal at SIXTYFOUR laid out the whole rig in FieldLux first: all 15 projectors, in four blend groups.

The aim was to work out how much light would land on each surface and where the projectors would overlap, before install day.

04 / THE HEATMAPS

Light, brightness and pixel density, surface by surface.

Heatmaps showed where the light would land, how bright it would be, and how much pixel density each wall and the floor would get.

In FieldLux these are the maps of the Heatmap overlay: Surface Lux, Luminance and Pixel Density, read on the model itself.

How to read them: Chapters 09 to 11

05 / THE CHECK

MEASURED AFTER OPENING.
THE NUMBERS HELD.

After the room opened, Neal went back with a light meter and measured. The numbers held.

That is the job FieldLux is for: settle the big questions before install day, so time on site goes to fine-tuning.

FieldLux values are optics-based planning estimates, not lux-meter readings. Check critical values on site with a meter, as SIXTYFOUR did.
