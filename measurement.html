Read the light before load-in.

What you can read

Three values. Three different questions.

Choose the map that answers your question. Lux, image scale and surface brightness are not interchangeable.

Surface Lux

How much light lands here?

Read illuminance in lx at 13 probes or under the cursor, for every projector that reaches the point or one head at a time. Pick the wall it is measured against with the Measurement target dropdown.

Pixel Density

How large is each pixel?

Read image scale on the target in mm/px: beam width divided by horizontal resolution, so resolution stays tied to the physical surface size.

Luminance

How bright does the surface appear?

Read surface brightness in cd/m2 from the material you set: reflectance and screen gain are editable in the Material window, with 21 presets from white wall to rear-projection screen.

Heatmap

Three scales for the same lux map.

Use the same scene to compare absolute values, reference ratios and percentage deviation.

13pt Diagnostic

Read the distribution, not just the center.

Press Values in the Heatmap panel: nine ANSI points and four quarter-points appear on the target with their readings, plus a center, average and uniformity summary with a grade. Move a projector and the probes follow.

What the numbers mean

See what the estimate uses.

FieldLux calculates from published projector and lens data plus the geometry and materials in your scene. It is an optics-based planning estimate, not a lux-meter reading.

Inputs

Geometry and equipment set the result

Lumens, lens throw range, distance, surface angle, overlap and material produce the displayed value. Ambient light does not change lux; it feeds the contrast ratio.

Basis

The calculation basis stays visible

The Heatmap panel header shows Physics Model, Spec-based or Calibrated, and the details show a Physics estimate badge. Epson and Panasonic lux is cross-checked against the manufacturers' own calculators and labelled Center Illuminance when it is.
