Part A · Start · Chapter 01

Sign in and open a scene.

Where: app.field-lux.com, then the page headed Projects.

  1. Open app.field-lux.com and sign in with your email and password, or choose Continue with Google. To create an account, choose Create account, fill in the form and your role, click Email verification code, then enter the six-digit code from the email.
  2. The Projects page opens with three tiles: New Project, Open FieldLux Test Scene and Open Play Dock Demo. Saved projects are listed below them. Delete moves a project to Trash, where you can restore it for 30 days.
  3. Click Open FieldLux Test Scene to get a working four-projector facade without importing anything. The copy is yours; the shared source never changes.
  4. Click New Project, type a project name when asked and click OK. The blank project is saved under that name and opens in the editor. Press Ctrl+O at any time to come back to Projects.

You will see: the editor with the menu bar (File, Edit, View, Modeling, Projector, Lighting, Tools, Appearance, Share, Settings, Help) with the project name and save status at its right end, Scene Layers on the left and the Inspector on the right. The Open beta model storage meter on the Projects page shows the 2 GB per-account limit.

Part A · Venue · Chapter 02

Import a 3D model.

Where: File > Import 3D Model (Ctrl+I), or Tools > Quick Target > Import 3D Model.

  1. Open File, choose Import 3D Model and select one .fbx, .obj, .glb, .dae, .stl or .ply file. For .gltf, select its .bin file and textures at the same time, or export a single .glb instead.
  2. Wait while the import progress window runs. A short pop-up message then reports how the unit was detected and, if the file does not state its up axis, reminds you to check Up axis.
  3. The model keeps the coordinates it was authored with and is selected. In the Inspector, open 3D Model to check File, Size, Vertices, Source unit and Dimensions.
  4. Check Source Orientation. A file exported Y-up, like this one, stands on the ground grid as it is. If a model lies on its side, click Z-up there (Blender, 3ds Max) or X-up, then click Center on bbox under Origin to centre it on the scene origin.

You will see: the mesh where its file places it, the Inspector header reading the file name with the tag MODEL SETTINGS, and an amber Unit may be wrong note if the largest dimension is under 0.1 m or over 200 m. Files of 100 MB or more ask for confirmation; the cap is 200 MB.

Part A · Venue · Chapter 03

Floor plans and sketches.

Where: File > Import Floor Plan, Modeling > Import CAD Drawing, or Modeling > New Sketch.

  1. Open File, choose Import Floor Plan and add a .dwg, .dxf, PDF, PNG or JPG. A DWG is converted in the browser, and a multi-sheet drawing asks which sheet you want.
  2. In Set the scale, click Use this to accept the unit read from the file, or click two points a known distance apart, type the real length and press Enter. For an image, type its overall width or height and press Enter; for a PDF you can also pick 1:50, 1:100 or 1:200.
  3. Click Import as reference lines. The plan appears in 3D as locked reference lines. For an image or PDF, click Place in 3D scene instead; it lands on the ground at the size you typed.
  4. The import opens the drawing in edit mode (later, select it and press Tab, or choose Modeling > Edit Linework). Press 2, click one line of the wall face you want to build from and Ctrl+click the rest of it, then click Trace selected to my sketch in the Build group. For a room, pick the inner face all the way round: a closed ring is what tells FieldLux which side is inside.
  5. With the traced lines selected, set Wall height and Wall thickness in the Inspector's Cleanup section, press W, then Enter. A closed ring rises as one room whose walls face inward; they receive projector light, block it from whatever is behind them and cast fixture shadows.
  6. Press Tab to leave edit mode, open Projector > Projector Library and click Add on a model. In the Inspector's Transform section type its Position to stand it inside the room. The image lands on the wall in front of the lens, with its lux and size labels.

You will see: a full-screen Import Floor Plan overlay with a scale chip, then a linework row in Scene Layers; in edit mode the top strip becomes the Modelling tools toolbar (Select, Draw, Modify, Cleanup, Build) and the prompt strip at the bottom describes the next click. A run that does not close, such as one with a doorway gap, has no inside to face, and the pop-up message after W says so: if its walls render black under a projector, select them and click Modeling > Flip Normals.

No drawing? Open Modeling and choose New Sketch; the Draw tool is already on. Click to place points, type a length such as 5m and press Enter to add that point, press Enter again to finish the run, then press W and Enter to raise the walls.

Part A · Venue · Chapter 04

Quick targets and LED walls.

Where: Tools > Quick Target (Basic Room, Flat Surface, Background Photo, Curved Wall, Cylinder, Dome / Sphere) and Tools > Add > LED Wall.

  1. Click Tools, then Basic Room under Quick Target. A 10 x 10 x 4 m room named Room 1 appears on the scene origin with an open top; type Width, Depth and Height in Room Dimensions. To add a ceiling that receives and blocks light, click the ceiling button at the lower left of the viewport.
  2. Click Tools, then Flat Surface for a single projection wall (5.0 x 2.81 m by default). Edit Width and Height in Dimensions, and click Flip normal in the Facing row if it renders black.
  3. For a dome, cylinder or curved wall, pick it under Quick Target and edit its Dimensions section: Width, Height and Curvature radius for a curved wall; Radius, Height and Arc for a cylinder; Radius and Coverage for a dome. Set Facing so the lit side faces the projectors.
  4. For a site photo, click Tools, then Background Photo, pick an image, and set Physical Width in the Inspector so the photo is at real scale. Raise Dimmer to darken the photo so projected light reads more clearly.
  5. Click Tools, then LED Wall under Add. In Add LED Wall, name it, click a preset such as Touring LED Wall P3.9, set Target Width and Target Height, and click Add LED Wall.

You will see: each target standing on the grid with its dimension labels; for the LED wall, a cabinet-tiled mesh and an Inspector reading Actual size, Cabinet count, Native res, Effective brightness in nits, Weight and Power, with Pixel risk, Washout and Camera risk badges under Analysis.

Part B · Projectors · Chapter 05

Projector Library and custom heads.

Where: Projector > Projector Library, and Projector > Custom Projector.

  1. Click Projector, then Projector Library. Pick a brand tab: Epson, Panasonic or Christie. Type in the search field to filter by model, series, resolution or lumens.
  2. On the card you want, choose a lens in the Lens dropdown (fixed-lens heads show none), then click Add. The projector appears in the scene, selected, and the Inspector opens on Projector Settings.
  3. To swap equipment later, select the projector and change Brand, Model, Lens or Aspect Ratio in the Equipment section. Lens shift is re-clamped to the new lens's official limits.
  4. For a head that is not in the library, click Projector, then Custom Projector. Type a name, pick a Resolution, set Brightness (ANSI Lumens) and the body size from the spec sheet, choose a Projection Lens Profile, and set Throw Ratio and the starting vertical and horizontal lens shift.
  5. Tick Save to Saved Custom Projectors for reuse before clicking Add Custom Projector. Next time it is one click under the Saved Custom Projectors tab.

You will see: library cards showing the model, lumens, resolution and throw-ratio range. In the Optics section the lux row reads Center Illuminance for Epson and Panasonic pairs backed by the manufacturer's calculator, and Center Surface Lux for Christie and custom heads.

Part B · Projectors · Chapter 06

Place, aim and set the optics.

Where: Edit > Edit Mode with the Move Tool (W) and Rotate Tool (E); Inspector > Projector Settings > Transform and Optics.

  1. Click a projector in the 3D view or in Scene Layers. Click Edit, then Edit Mode. Press W for the move gizmo and drag an arrow; press E for the rotate gizmo and drag a ring to aim. Press F to frame the selection.
  2. For exact placement, open Transform in the Inspector and type Position X, Y, Z in the display unit, mm by default (Y is height above the ground) and Rotation in degrees. Hover a box and click the padlock to lock that axis.
  3. Open Optics. Drag the Throw Ratio slider; its end labels are the real limits of the lens you chose. Beam W and Beam H follow: width equals distance divided by throw ratio.
  4. Read the distance row, Center Illuminance or Center Surface Lux in lx, Surface Tilt in degrees and Pixel Density in mm/px. Hover the question mark beside a row for its definition.
  5. At the bottom of Optics, drag V Shift and H Shift to move the image without tilting the projector. The limits are the lens's own. Click Reset Shift to Home to return.
  6. Use Projector Controls at the top of the panel to switch Power or Beam Preview off, or click Lock position so the projector cannot be moved.

You will see: a red, green and blue gizmo on the projector, the beam frustum and its W and H labels moving on the surface, and the Optics readouts updating each time you finish a move or confirm a value. A head whose catalogue lists a distance range, such as the Epson EB-800F, gets an amber Out-of-spec diagnostic model card with struck-through values when placed outside it.

Part B · Projectors · Chapter 07

Placement Tool arrays.

Where: Projector > Placement Tool, or the Projector array button in the viewport tool rail with a projector selected.

  1. Select the projector you want to replicate, then click Projector, then Placement Tool. The floating Projector Placement Tool window opens with the selected projector named at the top.
  2. Choose Linear, Circular or Symmetry. For Linear, set Count, Spacing, Axis and whether the selected projector is First, Center or Last. For Circular, set Count, Radius, Arc and Aim. For Symmetry, set the Mirror axis, Plane and Gap.
  3. Keep Live ghost preview ticked. Ghost projectors appear in the 3D view and in the Coordinate Table, which lists X, Y and Z for every unit.
  4. Click Place projectors to create real, individually editable projectors, or Start Live to keep the copies linked as an array that follows the source. A Circular ring on the default Group center pivot follows its Array Pivot node instead. Bake a live array later with Bake array in the Inspector's Array section.
  5. To align heads to each other, Ctrl+Click two or more projectors, clicking the reference head last, then click Show projector match axes in the tool rail and X, Y, Z or XYZ under Move or Rotate.

You will see: ghost projectors and a Coordinate Table in the tool window, then the new projectors in Scene Layers. Two or more placed heads are put in a blend group automatically. Each live copy shows an Array section marked Copy in the Inspector, with Position set to Follows array and Equipment set to Same as source; set either to Own to change that copy alone.

Part B · Projectors · Chapter 08

Blend groups and edge blending.

Where: the chain-link button in the Scene Layers header, and Inspector > Projector Settings > Edge Blending.

  1. In Scene Layers, click one projector, then Ctrl+Click the others that overlap. Click the chain-link button in the Scene Layers header (Create blend group from selected projectors). The group appears colour-coded in the list.
  2. Select one member and scroll the Inspector to Edge Blending. A new group starts with Enable on, so click Auto-Blend; switching Enable back on later runs it for you.
  3. Auto-Blend measures where the beams overlap on the surface and turns on the matching edges, Left, Right, Top or Bottom, with their overlap percentages and the partner's name. Click Auto-Blend to run it again.
  4. Drag an edge slider to adjust the feather from 0 to 50 percent; the readout shows percent and the feather width in your display unit. Pick a Blend Curve: Linear, Gamma 2.2 or S-Curve.
  5. If you move a projector afterwards, the Stale badge appears. Click Recalculate. When an amber note on an edge card flags a feather or brightness mismatch, click its Mirror or Match brightness button.

You will see: four edge cards, with enabled edges turning cyan, and a soft feather across the overlap in the 3D view instead of a doubled bright band. Measured lux stays pre-blend and represents each head at full white.

Part C · Measurement · Chapter 09

Heatmap maps and scales.

Where: the flame icon (Heatmap) in the floating button column at the top-right of the viewport, or View > Heatmap Overlay, then the Heatmap panel that appears at the left of the viewport.

  1. With at least one projector and a target in the scene, click the flame icon at the top-right of the viewport, or click View, then Heatmap Overlay.
  2. In the Heatmap panel, click a map: Surface Lux (lx), Pixel Density (mm/px) or Luminance (cd/m²). The header chip and the ramp labels follow the map.
  3. With Surface Lux selected, click Absolute and type a ceiling in Max lux so the red end of the ramp matches your brightest reading.
  4. Click Norm and type the target in Ref lux. The ramp reads 0.0x to 2.0x, and the band from 0.9x to 1.1x marks the reference.
  5. Click Deviation and pick a band with the Scale buttons, plus or minus 10, 20 or 30 percent, to show how far each point sits from the reference.
  6. Drag the panel by its grip to park it anywhere. Read the basis badge in its header, Physics Model for every head in today's library, and hover it to see what feeds the estimate.

You will see: the projected surfaces recoloured, blue through green and yellow to red for Absolute, blue rising to white across the 0.9x to 1.1x band, then orange to red, for Norm, and blue-white-red centred on 0 percent for Deviation. The number fields keep their values the next time you open FieldLux.

Part C · Measurement · Chapter 10

Values, 13pt Diagnostic and Lens View.

Where: the Values and Lens View buttons in the Heatmap panel; the Measurement target dropdown at the top of the Inspector; the cursor itself.

  1. Turn the Heatmap overlay on and select exactly one projector. In the Heatmap panel click Values.
  2. Read the 13 probes drawn on the projected image: TL, TC, TR, ML, C, MR, BL, BC, BR are the nine ANSI points, and L25, R25, T25, B25 are the quarter-points. Each carries its reading in the current map's unit.
  3. Read the FieldLux Distribution card in the panel: C (centre), Avg (mean of the nine), U (minimum of 13 over the mean) and the grade S, A, B or Correction Required. Click the chevron at the left of the panel header, then under Distribution click Composite or a projector name to summarise all heads or one.
  4. Click Lens View. The camera moves onto the projector's line of sight and frames its image; click Scene View to return.
  5. Move the cursor over any surface the selected projector lights. The tooltip reads Surface lux and mm/px under the cursor, with no button to press.
  6. At the top of the Inspector, open Measurement target. Leave it on Auto, which follows the selected projector's beam, or pick a room wall such as Room 1 · Front, a surface, a photo, a model or a primitive. Ceiling is listed only while the room has its ceiling on. Every lux and mm/px reading, in the viewport and in the PDF, then uses that surface.

You will see: 13 dots joined by thin white lines with bold point names and values such as 312 lx, 1.02x or 2.45 mm/px depending on map and mode; the probes follow the projector as you drag it. The panel's Probe set row reads 13pt Diagnostic.

Part C · Measurement · Chapter 11

Materials and ambient contrast.

Where: Appearance > Material, or right-click an object in Scene Layers and choose Material; Appearance > Ambient Light, and View > Ambient Light Preview.

  1. Select the surface or model, open Appearance and choose Material. In the floating Material window, choose a Library preset such as White Wall, Concrete, Screen/Fabric, Rear Projection or White Scrim.
  2. Under Surface set Type, Base color and Glossiness. Under Brightness Response read Reflectance and set Screen gain. These change the Luminance map and the cd/m² readouts only, with two exceptions that change measured lux: the Mirror type reflects beams onto other surfaces, and see-through presets such as White Scrim and Rear Projection pass part of the beam to the surfaces behind them.
  3. Click Appearance again. Under Ambient Light pick a preset such as Venue (house lights) or Full Blackout, or type a lux value.
  4. Read the contrast line below it. FieldLux shows a verdict from Optimal (Full Blackout) to Not viable, plus the CR n:1 ratio calculated as peak projector lux plus ambient, divided by ambient.
  5. To see the venue lit at that ambient level, click View, then Ambient Light Preview. For a site photo, raise Dimmer in the photo's Inspector section to darken the photo behind the projection.

You will see: the surface change colour and gloss in the viewport, the Luminance map rescale with reflectance times gain over pi, and a coloured contrast verdict under Ambient Light. Ambient light is a display and contrast input; lux, mm/px and heatmap values do not change with it.

Part D · Content · Chapter 12

Play Dock, Output Setup and media.

Where: the Play Dock strip along the bottom of the viewport, its Output Setup and Media buttons, and the workflow row and transport bar inside the console.

  1. Aim every projector at its wall, floor or model, then choose Output Setup on the collapsed Play Dock strip. In an open console, the same sheet is Detect surfaces at step 2.
  2. Under Regions, where your beams land, click a tile to include or skip it, drag to reorder, shift-drag to merge. Choose Auto, One canvas or My groups, and click Rescan after re-aiming. Under Outputs check each projector's region, pick a Source video, then click Apply Setup.
  3. The console stays open after Apply Setup (otherwise click the Play Dock strip): the workflow steps 1 Outputs to 6 Show along the top, the outputs or surfaces list on the left, the mapping editor in the centre, inspector tabs on the right and the transport at the bottom.
  4. Choose Media in the header. Drag a built-in loop from Patterns, or one of your uploads from Library, onto a wall in the 3D view or the transport bar. Choose Library to open the full Media Library and upload images or video, with a 1 GB limit per account.
  5. Press Play, or Space. Open the content chip's menu and click Add clip to build a playlist; use the loop button to cycle Clip, Playlist and Hold. Click Calibration to project a test pattern from every head of the group.
  6. In the centre pane, drag the cyan canvas to move the content on the face, drag its corners to resize, or type X, Y, W and H in the Canvas fields. Use Fill face or Fit ratio, and when a head runs past the canvas, click Fit canvas to beams in the amber note.

You will see: an Output Setup sheet with region tiles sized in metres, then a console whose header reads the focused surface, the current step and the slots count, above a workflow row that ticks steps 1 to 4 as you finish them. The mapping editor shows a to-scale plan of the face with every head's footprint. Play Dock previews the show in 3D; it does not output to hardware.

Part D · Content · Chapter 13

Warp & Blend and test patterns.

Where: the Play Dock console: click 4 Soft Edge or 5 Masks in the workflow row, or Warp & Blend above the mapping editor. An ungrouped projector opens the studio directly.

  1. Open the Play Dock console, click 4 Soft Edge in the workflow row and pick a surface in the list on the left. From steps 1 to 3, click Warp & Blend above the mapping editor.
  2. Click a numbered head chip to pick the projector to correct. All shows every head's outline read-only.
  3. For Corner-pin, drag the four cyan corners to keystone the picture and enable Perspective for a true homography. For Mesh, set the Grid columns and rows, drag the amber interior points and choose Spherize for domes.
  4. For a Mask, choose New mask and click points in the viewport to draw a feathered polygon, then set Invert and Feather. For Blend, choose Auto-Blend or adjust the left, right, top and bottom sliders, then tune Power, Gamma and Sharpness.
  5. Under Calibration, choose grid, crosshair, greyscale or colorbars to display a test pattern on the selected head, or choose All heads for the whole group. Choose None, show content to restore the picture. This is a pattern preview, not an automatic field-calibration solver. On the Corner-pin, Mesh or Mask tab, choose Reset to clear that correction.

You will see: a 2D studio of the head's frame with drag handles, and every corner-pin, mesh warp, mask and soft edge rendering live on the 3D beam. The footer reminds you that measurements never move: warp and blend change the picture, not the lux.

Part E · Lighting · Chapter 14

Lighting fixtures and Working Plane.

Where: the Lighting menu (Add Fixture, My Fixtures, Lighting Panel, Working Plane (Isolux)) and the Light Settings panel in the Inspector.

  1. Click Lighting, then a type under Add Fixture: Moving Head Spot, Moving Head Wash, Moving Head Beam, LED PAR / Static Wash, Profile / Ellipsoidal, Fresnel / PC, Flood / Cyc / Blinder or Strobe / Flash. The fixture appears, selected, and the Inspector switches to Light Settings.
  2. In the Fixture section pick a source: Quick Type, Catalog (29 datasheet classes), Library, GDTF / MVR (click Import .gdtf or .mvr and pick a .gdtf for this fixture or an .mvr for the whole rig) or Manual. In Photometrics enter Output in lumens and Beam Angle; read Peak intensity and the lux at the reference distance, both marked estimate.
  3. In Beam Preview switch on Beam visible and Light + shadows. Click Import .ies / .ldt to load the manufacturer's photometry. Aim with Pan, Tilt and Dimmer under Position & Aim, or drag the aim handle in 3D.
  4. Reopen Lighting and click Save Selected Fixture. It is listed under My Fixtures in every project you open in this browser.
  5. Expand Working Plane (Illuminance), set Plane height and switch on Show isolux in 3D. Read Average, Minimum, Maximum and Uniformity U0, and pick a Target profile for a pass or fail judgment. Under Looks (cues) type a name and click Save look; under Fixture schedule (BOM) click Copy schedule (CSV).

You will see: a luminaire model with a beam cone and aim handle, a coloured isolux plane over the lit task area at the chosen height, and a stats box labelled estimate with U0 green at 0.4 or above. Fixtures are a context layer: they never touch the projector lux formula.

Part E · Lighting · Chapter 15

Fog and volumetric beams.

Where: Lighting > Atmosphere > Fog Machine, and Appearance > Volumetric Beams.

  1. Click Lighting, then Fog Machine under Atmosphere. A machine appears on the scene origin, selected, and the Inspector shows the Fog Machine section. Type its Position, or drag it, to place it where the beams pass.
  2. Set Output, Fluid rate, Fluid (Quick dissipating, Standard or Long lasting), Plume spread, Plume reach and Nozzle velocity. Read Fog output in cubic metres per minute and grams per second.
  3. Click Appearance. Under Volumetric Beams make sure the toggle reads On. If no machine exists, the panel says to add one first.
  4. Adjust Wind, Direction, Gust and Ventilation to move and clear the cloud. Pick Quality (Low, Medium or High) and Beam visibility.
  5. Orbit the scene. Projector and fixture beams now glow in the air and projected content is carried through the fog.

You will see: a plume from the nozzle, visible light shafts from up to four projectors and four fixtures (the brightest and nearest to the camera), and haze-attenuated walls. Measured lux does not change; the "with fog (est.)" readout is clearly marked as an estimate.

Part F · Deliver · Chapter 16

Save, versions and project files.

Where: File > Save (Ctrl+S), Save As (Ctrl+Shift+S), Version history, Export Project File and Open Project File; Settings > Auto Save.

  1. Press Ctrl+S or open File and choose Save. A project started with New Project is already in the cloud under its name. The chip at the right end of the menu bar reads Saving while it works, then the Unsaved dot disappears. Click the project name beside it to rename the project, or choose Save As to name a copy.
  2. Click Settings and drag the Auto Save slider between 1 and 30 minutes. While the scene has unsaved changes, FieldLux saves a version to the cloud at that interval and keeps a draft in your browser.
  3. Open File and choose Version history. Hover over an older row, choose Restore and confirm. The selected version is restored as a new save, so your current state remains in the history.
  4. Open File and choose Export Project File to download an editable .flx.json. On any machine or account, choose Open Project File to load it, then press Ctrl+S to store it in the cloud.

You will see: pop-up messages reading Saved, Saved as new project or Restored, saved as a new version; a Version history window listing saves by time with the newest tagged Current; and a downloaded project.flx.json. Imported 3D models are not copied into the file: it points to the copies stored in the cloud. If another tab or device saved the project after you opened it, the chip warns that it changed elsewhere; click it to choose Reload latest, Save as a copy or Overwrite.

Part F · Deliver · Chapter 17

Share links.

Where: Share > Share opens Share 3D View; Share > Share Management lists every link.

  1. Open Share and choose Share. In the Cloud Share block, enter a Link label, choose an expiry of 2, 7, 14 or 30 days or Never, and enable Require a password when needed.
  2. Under What viewers can do, toggle Allow PDF Tech Rider download, Show my measurements and Allow walk mode.
  3. Click Create Cloud Share. Click Copy next to the URL, or let the recipient scan the QR code under Scan to open. Recipients open a read-only viewer without signing in.
  4. For a small scene without imported 3D models, the URL Share block packs the scene into the link itself; click Generate URL Share and Copy. Nothing is uploaded. A large rig needs a Cloud Share.
  5. To give a colleague's FieldLux account read-only project access, type their email under FieldLux Account Share and click Share. The project appears in their Projects list, read-only.
  6. Open Share and choose Share Management to copy, extend or revoke a link; once revoked, a link can be deleted. Extensions are available for 2, 7, 14 or 30 days, or permanently.

You will see: the generated URL with a Copy button and a QR code, a pop-up message reading Cloud share created, and in Share Management every link grouped as Active or Expired or revoked with its time left. A revoked link tells the recipient the owner revoked it.

Part F · Deliver · Chapter 18

PDF Tech Rider.

Where: File > Export PDF Tech Rider. Also Ctrl+K, then type Export PDF.

  1. Open the set-up project you want to document and frame the 3D viewport the way you want it captured. Turn the Heatmap overlay on if you want the lux legend in the PDF.
  2. Click File, then Export PDF Tech Rider. The row greys out while the export runs, and for a large rig a progress message with a Cancel button may appear.
  3. Open FieldLux_Tech_Rider_date.pdf from your downloads.

You will see: page 1 with the viewport capture, a PROJECT SETUP table (projectors, total lumens, target) and a PROJECTOR SETTINGS INDEX, then one page per projector with EQUIPMENT, LENS, POSITION & ORIENTATION, OPTICAL DISTANCES, SURFACE SAMPLING and, for heads in a blend group, EDGE BLENDING. A disclaimer band reads that it is a simulation preview, not a field measurement.

Part F · Deliver · Chapter 19

Settings, shortcuts and walk mode.

Where: the Settings and View menus, Help > Keyboard Shortcuts (F1), Tools > Search Commands (Ctrl+K), and the G key.

  1. Click Settings, then Display Unit to cycle mm, m and ft. Every dimension field and label follows.
  2. Click View to show or hide the Layers and Inspector panels, switch between the Design (Ctrl+1) and Simulate (Ctrl+2) workspaces, or jump to a camera preset from Top to Free 3D.
  3. Press Ctrl+K and type part of any command, for example toggle heat for Toggle Heatmap Overlay or front for the Front view. Enter runs the highlighted row.
  4. Press G to walk through the room in first person; Esc leaves walk mode. Press F to frame the selection.
  5. Press F1 for the Keyboard Shortcuts sheet. Under Settings, set Auto Save, toggle Depth Occlusion and Projector Shadows, and pick the Occlusion Quality.

You will see: the Display Unit hint changing between mm, m and ft; the command palette as a floating panel with grouped sections and shortcut hints; and the Keyboard Shortcuts sheet grouped as Anywhere, Viewport, Walking through a room and the drawing groups.

Part G · Immersive · Chapter 20

One video across walls and floor.

Where: Play Dock > 2 Surfaces > Detect surfaces…, or Output Setup… on the collapsed dock. Both open the Output Setup window.

  1. Start with a room and its projectors. Choose Tools > Basic Room, set its Height under Room Dimensions and leave Ceiling Lid off. Add projectors from Projector > Projector Library and aim them so every face is covered by overlapping beams, each beam inside its own face. Specs can be mixed: here each wall gets two 20,000 lm heads on a zoom lens from above the opposite wall, and the floor gets six 16,000 lm short-throw heads pointing straight down in a 2 × 3 grid. Leave them out of any blend group.
  2. Click the flame icon at the top-right of the viewport to turn the Heatmap on and check the coverage before you blend, with Max lux set above the overlap level (2,500 lx here). Every face should read lit edge to edge, and the overlaps show as brighter bands: one down the middle of each wall and a grid across the floor. Click the icon again to turn it off.
  3. Click PLAY DOCK at the bottom of the screen to open the dock, click 2 Surfaces, then click Detect surfaces….
  4. Check the regions. With no groups, the layout is Auto: it finds one surface per face from where the beams land, here five screens: the floor with six projectors and each wall with two. Clicking a tile skips that surface.
  5. Under Video across regions, choose Floor-centred — content runs off the floor and up every wall.
  6. Under Source, pick a video. This example uses the bundled 4K UHD (3840 x 2160) clip.
  7. Click Apply Setup. One blend group per face, each face's crop of the video, the edge blend inside every face with more than one projector, and playback are all set in one undo step.
  8. Pause the clip with the Pause button in the dock's transport bar and check every face. Rings that bend the wrong way, or content that breaks at the floor edge, mean that face's panel is turned the wrong way. In this room the front and back walls came out upside down.
  9. Click 3 Input to see the master canvas: the floor in the middle, with each wall folded out from the edge it stands on. Click the chip of the panel to fix (S4 is the front wall here) and press ⟳ 90° until it reads right. Each press turns only that panel a quarter turn, so an upside-down wall takes two.
  10. Fix the rest the same way, one panel at a time: S3, the back wall, takes two presses, and S1, the floor, takes one so its crop runs the same way as the folded walls.
  11. Press Play, then click 4 Soft Edge. Every face reads blended: each wall across its two projectors, and the floor across its six in both directions.
  12. Press ` to collapse the dock, select the room in Scene Layers and press G. Drag to look around, use W A S D to move, and press Esc to leave.

You will see: one clip running off the floor and up all four walls, sent to all fourteen projectors from one video. Projectors that share a face are edge-blended automatically, the floor grid along both its columns and its rows. Once every panel faces the right way, the four wall-to-floor edges are continuous; the four vertical corners meet edge to edge without a blend, the way real immersive rooms are built. Floor-centred never uses a ceiling, and a 16:9 clip is stretched to fill the square master canvas.
