The workflow

Build the venue. Place the system. Share the plan.

Two workspaces, one scene. Design holds the venue, Simulate holds the projectors, lighting and blend, and every number follows the geometry you are looking at.

01 · Design

Build the venue

Import a 3D model (OBJ, FBX, GLB, glTF, DAE, STL, PLY) with unit and up-axis detection, load a DWG, DXF, PDF or image floor plan at scale and raise its walls, or start from a Basic Room, Flat Surface, Curved Wall, Cylinder, Dome or a site photo. Everything sits at real scale on the ground grid.

02 · Projector Library

Choose the projector and lens

Pick an Epson, Panasonic or Christie head and a lens from its compatible list, or build a custom head from any spec sheet and save it for reuse. Epson and Panasonic lux readouts are cross-checked against the manufacturers' own calculators.

03 · Placement Tool

Place, aim and blend the rig

Set throw ratio and lens shift inside the lens's real range, then build linear, circular or symmetry arrays that stay live as you move the source. Group the heads, run Auto-Blend, and set the overlap per edge in percent and metres.

04 · Heatmap

Read the surface

Turn on the Heatmap overlay for Surface Lux, Pixel Density or Luminance, press Values for the 13pt Diagnostic, and check the projection-to-ambient contrast ratio. Every readout is a physics estimate, labelled as one.

05 · Play Dock

Preview the content

Open the Play Dock, let Output Setup find the screens from your beams, drop images or video from the Media Library, and corner-pin, mesh-warp and soft-edge each head in Warp & Blend. Play Dock previews the show in 3D. It does not output it.

06 · Share and handoff

Hand off the plan

Export a PDF Tech Rider in one click, save an editable .flx.json project file, or create a read-only Cloud Share link with a QR code, optional password and expiry you can revoke later. Projects are cloud-saved with autosave and version history.

Lighting and atmosphere

Put the fixtures and the fog in the same scene.

Projection never plays alone. Place the lighting rig next to the projectors and see what each one does to the other.

How to do it: Chapters 14 and 15

Fixtures

Eight fixture types, or your own

Add a Moving Head Spot, Wash or Beam, LED PAR, Profile, Fresnel, Flood or Strobe from the Lighting menu, pick a catalogue class, or import GDTF and MVR files. Load IES or LDT photometry for the real beam. Tune one and save it to My Fixtures for every project.

Working Plane

Isolux on a plane you set

Show the isolux working plane at any height, read average, minimum, maximum and uniformity, save fixture states as Looks, and copy the fixture schedule as CSV for the rider.

Fog

Beams you can see in the air

Add a Fog Machine, set output, fluid and plume, then dial wind, gust and ventilation under Appearance. Projector and fixture beams glow through the haze. Measured lux does not change with fog.

Read what lands

Surface Lux, Pixel Density and Luminance, on the model.

Compare Absolute, Norm and Deviation maps, probe any point under the cursor, and read one head or the composite. Directly on the projected surfaces in the scene.
