WHO THIS IS FOR

BUILT FOR THE PERSON WHO QUOTES THE JOB.

FieldLux answers how many projectors, which lens, from where, and how bright. In the browser, before anyone signs. It is not a media server, it is not a rig warping tool, and nothing authored here is ever sent to a device. It is the planning step, done properly.

  • (1)

    BRING THE VENUE IN

    Import a model, or build the room in the app. Then place projectors from a catalogue built off real spec sheets.

  • (2)

    MEASURE THE LIGHT

    Lux heatmaps on the surface, a probe under the cursor, and a 13-point probe set drawn on the target.

  • (3)

    HAND OFF THE PROOF

    A PDF Tech Rider, a neutral handoff JSON and a read-only link. Each one states its own assumptions.

(01) THE SCENE

ONE SCENE HOLDS THE WHOLE JOB.

The venue, every projector, the lens on each one, and the surfaces you must not spill onto all live in the same scene. Move anything, and every number downstream recomputes from it.

  • SIX MODEL FORMATS

    FBX, GLB/GLTF, OBJ, DAE, STL and PLY, up to 200 MB per file. Units and up-axis are read on import, so a millimetre export lands at real size.

  • OR BUILD IT HERE

    Rooms, curved walls, cylinders and domes are one click each. No 3D software required.

  • BIG FILES STAY WORKABLE

    The reference test model is an FBX with 2,020 meshes in 571 groups. Imports are cleaned on the way in, so embedded lights and cameras are stripped.

  • WALK INSIDE

    Drop to eye level and walk the venue with the beams running.

(02) THE OPTICS

EVERY NUMBER COMES OFF A SPEC SHEET.

87 projectors and 113 lenses, typed in from the manufacturers' own PDFs. Every field keeps the URL of the sheet it was read from.

  • THROW RATIO, LIVE

    Drag the throw ratio and the beam size, axial distance and Center Surface Lux move together, inside the lens's real range.

  • LENS SHIFT THAT PUSHES BACK

    Shift is clamped to each lens's published travel. Past a quarter of that travel, edge falloff is modelled instead of ignored.

  • CROSS-CHECKED, 642 TIMES

    642 projector-and-lens combinations verified against Panasonic's and Epson's own calculators, the only two that can be queried. Pulled 2026-06-11.

  • OUT OF SPEC SAYS SO

    Place a head where its lens cannot reach and the beam and lux cards strike through, rather than print a figure it cannot hit.

(03) THE COVERAGE

MANY HEADS, ONE CONTINUOUS IMAGE.

Auto edge blend measures where each beam actually lands and writes the per-edge overlap widths and the blend curve for you. One undo step reverses all of it. The values are light-linear planning values for the study, never settings pushed to a processor.

  • MEASURED FOOTPRINTS

    Overlaps come from each head's real optical frame, lens shift included, not from an idealised rectangle.

  • THE 10 TO 30% BAND

    Overlaps outside the industry 10 to 30 percent band get a named warning, not silence.

  • DOMES INCLUDED

    Curved surfaces are measured with 360 rays per head on the actual mesh, never flattened into a plane first.

  • STALE MEANS FLAGGED

    Move a head a centimetre, or half a degree, and its blend is marked stale until you recalculate.

ALL 5 CLIPS FOR THIS PART ↗

(04) THE CORRECTION

CORRECTION YOU CAN WATCH IN THE BEAM.

Corner-pin, mesh warp and feathered masks render in the actual 3D beam while you drag, so the seam softens in the viewport in front of you. Up to 32 heads can carry a mesh warp or mask, and your on-site correction still happens on the processor.

  • CORNER-PIN

    Four handles, true perspective math, live in the viewport. The 2D preview and the 3D render share the same equations.

  • MESH WARP

    A subdividable grid, 8 × 6 by default, with a Spherize helper for domes.

  • FEATHERED MASKS

    Click-drawn polygons with feather, invert and stacking, cut into the beam itself.

  • CALIBRATION PATTERNS

    Grid, crosshair, greyscale ramp and colorbars, projected live per head, even while a video is playing.

ALL 4 CLIPS FOR THIS PART ↗

(05) THE CONTENT

DRAG IN 2D. THE BEAMS FOLLOW IN 3D.

The Mapping Stage is a zoomable 2D view of the projection face. Move the canvas and the 3D beams track it live. The parts of a beam that miss the canvas draw as cut, with a one-click fix.

  • VIDEO AND STILLS

    Per blend group or per single head, with playlists, auto-advance and loop modes.

  • FOUR SOURCES, SAID OUT LOUD

    The budget is four distinct content sources at once. The fifth drop is refused with a message, before anything is written, rather than failing quietly on the GPU.

  • ONE VIDEO, MANY SURFACES

    Canvas sets span one video across several surfaces as proportional strips, or wrap a dome so every head samples the same picture.

ALL 3 CLIPS FOR THIS PART ↗

(06) THE MEASUREMENT

THE SURFACE BECOMES A LIGHT METER.

Three data modes paint the surface in false colour: Surface Lux in lx, Pixel Density in mm/px, and Luminance in cd/m². Add a probe under the cursor and a 13-point probe set drawn on the target in world space. Every figure is computed from the projector specs and the scene geometry, never from a field measurement, and the error each assumption carries is set out under Limits.

ALL 5 CLIPS FOR THIS PART ↗

(07) THE LIGHT

LIGHT BEHAVES LIKE LIGHT.

Projector light adds to the surface's own shading: a white beam brightens a dark wall and the texture stays visible. Beams stop at the first surface they hit, so occlusion and shadows are part of the picture.

  • ADDITIVE, ALWAYS

    Black in the content means no light: the wall simply stays as it was. Nothing is painted over.

  • AMBIENT PRESETS

    From 100,000 lx direct sunlight down to 5 lx theatre, plus full blackout. The contrast ratio is judged against whichever you set.

  • AN ESTIMATE, LABELLED

    Beyond direct light the model stops at a single-bounce room estimate. That carries the widest error budget in the product, and it is published under Limits.

ALL 3 CLIPS FOR THIS PART ↗

(08) THE ROOM AROUND IT

PROJECTION NEVER SHIPS ALONE.

Stage and architectural fixtures and LED walls sit in the same scene as the projectors, eight archetypes deep, with IES (LM-63), EULUMDAT and GDTF import and a washout rating for the wall. All of it lives in a strictly separate estimate lane: direct light only, stamped estimate on screen, and blocked from ever reaching a projector lux figure.

  • THE ISOLUX WORKING PLANE

    Eavg, Emin, Emax and uniformity on a plane at any height. Direct light only, and walls actually block it.

  • IES IMPORT

    A manufacturer .ies, .ldt or .gdtf file drives the beam shape, the fixture's lux estimate and the working plane. Asymmetric files are averaged, and the app says so on screen.

  • AIM BY CLICK

    Click a point on the target and pan and tilt solve themselves, inside the fixture's real travel.

  • A CATALOGUE, NOT JUST A SHAPE

    29 built-in fixtures, 16 of them named market units transcribed from published photometric data, on top of the eight archetypes. For the exact curve, drop the maker's own file on top.

  • FOG YOU CAN SEE THE BEAM IN

    A fog machine puts a simulated field in the air that wind and ventilation move and walls contain, and the beams scatter in it. It is a display lane only: it adds no lux to a surface and changes no measured figure.

  • THE SCHEDULE LEAVES WITH YOU

    One click copies a fixture schedule with per-unit specs and the total power draw.

(09) THE DELIVERABLE

PROOF THAT LEAVES THE APP.

What leaves is a PDF Tech Rider, a vendor-neutral handoff JSON, and a read-only link. Each one states its own assumptions on the page. None of it is written in a media server's blend or warp format, and none of it is pushed to a device.

  • PDF TECH RIDER, ONE CLICK

    One page per projector: equipment, lens, position, optical distances, surface readings and per-edge blending, all under a printed “simulation preview, not a field measurement” band.

  • HANDOFF JSON

    Scene, bindings, blend groups and the analysis summary in one neutral file, with the blend-domain warning printed inside the file itself.

  • A READ-ONLY LINK

    The recipient lands on your exact camera and can print the same Tech Rider in your units. The link is a dated receipt, not a live mirror. It can expire, and it can be revoked.

(10) THE PROJECT

THE WORK SURVIVES THE SESSION.

A local crash draft saves a second after you stop, and a cloud autosave writes on a schedule you set. Where a project cannot be fully revived, the app refuses to write rather than overwrite your last good save. That is still not an offline mode: signing in needs the network.

  • CLOUD AUTOSAVE

    Every save is a new version on an append-only version chain. Nothing is ever overwritten.

  • RESTORE ROLLS FORWARD

    Restoring first saves what you have, then writes the restored state as a new version. Nothing is deleted.

  • SAMPLE SCENES

    Open your own editable copy of the FieldLux Test Scene or the Play Dock Demo, and break it freely.

  • OR KEEP IT YOURSELF

    Export the whole project as a single file and open it again later. No cloud required.

LIMITS

WHAT WE ASSUME, AND WHAT IT COSTS.

Every constant the analysis runs on is frozen in one module, with the industry source it came from and the error it carries. It is all published here, because you should be able to read a number off this product and know exactly what was assumed to produce it.

(01) WHAT IS ASSUMED, AND WHAT EACH ASSUMPTION COSTS

ROOM BOUNCE

Single-bounce form factor, a heuristic rather than a standard. Error budget +/- 50 %, and the code says in as many words that it is for planning, not measurement. It is the worst budget in this table, which is why it is stated first.

SURFACE REFLECTANCE

0.60, ISO 9241-303 reference matte white. Error budget +/- 0.10 absolute, ordinary paint variance. If you know the paint that is actually on the surface, set a material preset instead.

PROJECTOR UNIFORMITY

ANSI 9-point: centre 1.00, edge 0.90, corner 0.85, average 0.92. Source ANSI/INFOCOMM 2M-2010. Error budget +/- 5 % per point while the head is inside its spec, +/- 15 % once it is outside.

SURFACE SAMPLING

16 x 16 = 256 points across the target. Error budget +/- 5 % on Reliable Surface Lux (full-target p20), falling to +/- 1 % at 32 x 32. The shipped grid is the 16 x 16 one.

THROW GEOMETRY

Raycast from the optical frame to the target. Error budget +/- 2 % on flat targets, +/- 15 % on imported models. On a model you did not build yourself, work to the +/- 15 % figure.

NON-PLANAR FALLBACK

Bounding-box centroid plane projection. Error budget +/- 15 to 30 %. It flattens the curve into a plane to get a number at all, so on a curved target carry the whole band, not the middle of it.

HOW THESE COMBINE

They do not. Each budget is declared on its own, and neither the app nor this page rolls them into a single accuracy figure for a run. A curved imported target lit through bounced light sits inside three of these bands at once, and how they interact is not modelled. Read them as separate warnings, not as one number to subtract.

(02) WHAT IS NOT MODELLED, ABSENT RATHER THAN APPROXIMATE

NOT MODELLED

Lens distortion, chromatic aberration, specular BRDF, ambient interaction with the surface and content-dependent brightness are not modelled at all. Surfaces are Lambertian only. There is no radiosity, no global illumination and no ray tracing anywhere in this product. Two items that used to sit on this list now carry a narrower absence instead: atmospheric haze renders as a display-only single-scattering pass and reads out as a labelled visibility estimate, and lamp aging enters the fixture estimate through a maintenance factor slider that is not saved with the scene. Neither writes back into measured projector lux, which stays clear-air and un-aged. The rest do not tighten with a longer run; they are absent from the calculation.

FIXTURE LIGHT IS A SEPARATE LANE

Stage and architectural fixture illuminance is stamped "estimate" in code and on screen: direct light only, no interreflection. That module is blocked from importing the projector photometry code at all, and a test checks its import list stays clean every run. A context estimate can never mix into the projector lux figure.

DIALUX-STYLE, NOT DIALUX-GRADE

The working-plane isolux calculation is described as DIALux-style. It is not a certified calculation and it is not a substitute for one.

IES CAVEAT

Asymmetric IES files are averaged into an axially symmetric approximation, and the app says so on screen. A fixture whose real distribution is not symmetric about its axis is estimated as though it were.

GRAZING FOLDS READ NARROW

Where two faces of a fold meet at a grazing angle, under about 35 degrees, the blend band is still computed as if the surface were flat. Pixel measurement puts the real spill 1.5 to 2.7 times wider than the model there. The app warns on the seam when it happens, and the number it shows you is the narrow one.

STANDARD TARGETS ARE REFERENCE VALUES

The working-plane numbers can be judged against target profiles for five regions, and every figure in those packs is a reference value transcribed from public secondary sources, not a licensed normative table. The app prints that qualifier beside the judgment, and this page repeats it: a pass against one of these profiles is a pass against our transcription of it.

(03) WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED

NO MEASURED VALIDATION

Nothing in this product has been compared against lux-meter field data. The budgets above are self-declared model uncertainty, not a measured accuracy figure. Every map carries its accuracy basis on its own legend: Physics Model (estimated), Spec-based (datasheet), Calibrated or Measured. We say "simulation preview" and "physics estimate" because that is what it is.

WHICH LUX, ALWAYS LABELLED

At least four lux figures with different meanings exist in the engine, and the file that owns them warns at the top that every past bug in this area came from mixing them. Reliable Surface Lux is a full-target 20th percentile. Not a peak, not an average. Every lux number on this site says which one it is in the same sentence, and quoting one without its label is how the same scene ends up with two brightness stories.

BLEND VALUES ARE LIGHT-LINEAR

FieldLux blend ramps are a partition of unity in luminous flux, and every export carrying them states that domain. Typed verbatim into a signal-space media-server blend panel they put roughly a -56 % dark band at seam centre. They are planning values, not processor settings.

NEVER UNDER LOAD

Production has never been load tested. A staging load test on 2026-08-29 did run concurrent snapshot saves, and the compressed save format that production has used since 2026-09-05 finished 64 out of 64 concurrent saves at a 238 ms 95th percentile with no failure inside that range, while the plain format it replaced failed 6 of 8 at eight concurrent saves. Staging is a smaller free-tier instance, so read both figures as a floor and not as a production number. The highest live traffic we have observed in production is 30 requests per second, measured across one day of edge logs on 2026-08-14 and not re-measured since. We publish the measured per-session request cost and those two floors and nothing else: no uptime figure, no concurrency guarantee.

THE PUBLIC-BUCKET CAVEAT

Model binaries and site photos referenced by a share are served from a public storage bucket. Anyone who has already resolved a share can keep downloading those files after you revoke, expire or password-protect the link. This is known, documented and deliberately deferred: revocation stops the link, it does not un-download a file.

WHAT'S IN BETA

GDTF and MVR fixture import is code-complete but not yet checked against real vendor files. The Report Builder client-proposal PDF sits behind a beta gate and is off in deployed builds. Both are labelled that way inside the app too.

WHAT ISN'T HERE

Realtime video output to other applications is desktop-only and its entry points are removed from the web build entirely. There is no export to a media-server-specific file format. There is no live multi-user editing, no comments and no presence. Sharing is owner-authored links plus read-only account access.

SECURITY, STATED NARROWLY

What exists: row-level security scoped to the project owner, share payloads readable only through a server-side resolver, bcrypt-hashed share passwords, IP-hashed rate limiting on share resolution and on both signup and founding-member verification, one-time signup codes stored only as a salted hash, per-user storage path isolation, admin-gated operations, a 7-day grace period on account deletion, and an explicit consent record at signup. That list is the whole claim.
