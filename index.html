(01) THE SIMULATION

BUILD THE VENUE. THE SURFACE READS THE LIGHT BACK.

The venue arrives as a model in 6 formats, as a floor plan traced at its real size, or as walls you draw and raise. Then the surface reports what lands on it: Surface Lux in lx, Pixel Density in mm/px and Luminance in cd/m² painted in false colour, a probe under the cursor, and a 13-point probe set drawn on the target. Every figure comes from the projector specs and the scene geometry, never from a field measurement.

  • SIX MODEL FORMATS

    FBX, GLB/GLTF, OBJ, DAE, STL and PLY, up to 200 MB per model file. Units and up-axis are read on import: a millimetre export lands at real size. Or build the room here: rooms, curved walls, cylinders and domes are one click each, and you can drop to eye level and walk it with the beams running.

  • FLOOR PLAN UNDERLAY, WALLS FROM LINES

    Drop a PNG, JPG or PDF page and set its real size by typing a dimension, clicking two points a known distance apart, or picking 1:50, 1:100 or 1:200 on a PDF plotted to scale. Trace the lines you want and they rise as walls at a height you type. A closed outline becomes a floor, and those walls are projection receivers like any other surface.

  • LIGHT BEHAVES LIKE LIGHT

    Projector light adds to the surface's own shading, so black in the content means no light and the wall stays as it was. Beams stop at the first surface they hit, so occlusion and shadows are part of the picture. Ambient runs from 100,000 lx direct sunlight down to 5 lx theatre plus full blackout, and beyond direct light the app labels its single-bounce room figure an estimate on screen. An LED wall in the scene carries brightness in nits, spill on it in lux and a washout risk rating for the wall, in every tier.

  • IES IMPORT (PRO)

    Stage and architectural fixtures sit in the same scene: 29 built-in units on 8 archetypes, 16 of them named market units transcribed from published photometric data. A manufacturer IES (LM-63), EULUMDAT or GDTF file drives the beam shape and the number. GDTF and MVR are code-complete but not yet checked against real vendor files, asymmetric files are averaged, and the app says both on screen. Aim by clicking the target, and copy a schedule with the total power draw.

  • THE ISOLUX WORKING PLANE (PRO)

    Eavg, Emin, Emax and uniformity on a plane at any height, against 38 target profiles across 5 regional standard sets, and walls actually block it. Add a fog machine and the beams scatter in air that ventilation moves and walls contain. Fixture light and fog are a strictly separate estimate lane: direct light only, stamped estimate on screen, and blocked from ever reaching a projector lux figure.

(02) THE ARRAY

PLACE THE HEADS. THE OVERLAPS COUNT THEMSELVES.

87 projectors and 113 lenses, typed in from the manufacturers' own PDFs, every field keeping the URL of the sheet it was read from. Place them one at a time or as a linear array, a circular array or a mirrored rig. Auto edge blend then measures where each beam actually lands and writes the per-edge overlap widths and the blend curve, and one undo reverses all of it.

  • THROW RATIO, LIVE

    Drag the throw ratio and the beam size, axial distance and Center Surface Lux move together, inside the lens's real range.

  • LENS SHIFT THAT PUSHES BACK

    Shift is clamped to each lens's published travel, and past a quarter of that travel edge falloff is modelled instead of ignored. Place a head where its lens cannot reach and the beam and lux cards strike through rather than print a figure it cannot hit.

  • ARRAYS THAT STAY LIVE

    Linear, circular and symmetry rigs stay live: edit the source head and every generated head follows it. Bake the rig when you need to edit the heads separately, and reassemble it later. A bake is refused above 200 projectors in a scene, which is a hard shader cap the runtime detector can set lower on your machine.

  • AUTO EDGE BLEND, MEASURED

    Overlaps come from each head's real optical frame, lens shift included, not from an idealised rectangle. Overlaps outside the industry 10 to 30 percent band get a named warning, not silence. Move a head a centimetre, or half a degree, and its blend is marked stale until you recalculate. The values are light-linear planning values, never settings pushed to a processor.

  • CROSS-CHECKED, 642 TIMES

    642 projector-and-lens combinations verified against Panasonic's and Epson's own calculators, the only two that can be queried, covering 47 of the 87 heads. Pulled 2026-06-11.

(03) THE PLAY DOCK (PRO)

DRAG IN 2D. THE BEAMS FOLLOW IN 3D.

The Play Dock is the show console: a bottom overlay in two states, never hidden, holding a Sets and Groups rail, a surface filmstrip, inspector cards and a transport with a ticking timecode. It is priced into Pro alongside the fixture lane and the fog machine, and unlike those two it has no switch in the build yet, so it opens for every account today. Video and stills on a single head, and edge blending, stay in Projection; Pro buys the group lane above them.

  • THE MAPPING STAGE (PRO)

    A zoomable 2D view of the projection face. Move the canvas and the 3D beams track it live. Parts of a beam that miss the canvas draw as cut, with a one-click fix.

  • CORNER-PIN, MESH WARP, FEATHERED MASKS (PRO)

    Four handles on true perspective math, a subdividable grid at 8 x 6 with a Spherize helper for domes, and click-drawn polygons with feather, invert and stacking cut into the beam itself. All three render in the actual 3D beam while you drag. Up to 32 heads can carry a mesh warp or a mask.

  • ONE VIDEO, MANY SURFACES (PRO)

    Canvas sets span one video across several surfaces as proportional strips. Quick Wrap builds those regions from where the light already lands, and wraps a dome so every head samples the same picture, measured with 360 rays per head on the actual mesh rather than flattened into a plane first.

  • PLAYLISTS AND CALIBRATION PATTERNS (PRO)

    Playlists with auto-advance and loop modes on a group, and a grid, crosshair, greyscale ramp or colorbars projected live per head, even while a video is playing.

  • FOUR SOURCES, SAID OUT LOUD

    The budget is four distinct content sources at once, and it is the same budget on a single head, so it applies in every tier. The fifth drop is refused with a message before anything is written, not failed quietly on the GPU.

  • NOT A MEDIA SERVER

    Nothing authored in the dock is sent to a device. It previews content so the beams show the right picture, and your on-site correction still happens on the processor.

WHAT LEAVES

PROOF THAT LEAVES THE APP.

Three things leave: a PDF Tech Rider, one page per projector with equipment, lens, position, optical distances, surface readings and per-edge blending under a printed “simulation preview, not a field measurement” band; a vendor-neutral handoff JSON carrying the blend-domain warning inside the file itself; and a read-only link that is a dated receipt rather than a live mirror, and can expire or be revoked. None is written in a media server's blend or warp format, and none is pushed to a device.

  • CLOUD AUTOSAVE, APPEND-ONLY

    Every save is a new version on an append-only version chain. Restoring first saves what you have, then writes the restored state as a new version, so nothing is overwritten and nothing is deleted. Or export the whole project as one file and keep it yourself.
