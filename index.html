What is in the box

in one scene.

These eight workflows are available today and free during open beta. Each has a step-by-step guide on the Videos page.

01 · Venue

Bring the venue in.

Import an OBJ, FBX, GLB, glTF, DAE, STL or PLY model; FieldLux detects its units and lets you fix the up axis in one click. Load a DWG, DXF, PDF or image floor plan at scale, trace the walls and raise them to height, or sketch the room from scratch. For a quick stand-in, add a Basic Room, Flat Surface, Curved Wall, Cylinder, Dome, site photo or LED wall from Tools.

How to do it: Chapters 02 to 04 ↗

02 · Projector Library

Real heads, real lenses.

Pick an Epson, Panasonic or Christie projector and a documented compatible lens. Epson and Panasonic lux readouts are cross-checked against the manufacturers' own calculators. For another model, create a custom head from its spec sheet and save it for future projects.

How to do it: Chapter 05 ↗

03 · Placement Tool

Arrays that stay live.

Build a linear run, a circular ring with a shared aim or a symmetry pair, then review the ghost preview and coordinate table. Choose Place projectors for independent copies or Start Live so the copies follow the source, and give any copy its own position or equipment. Line up position or rotation across selected heads from the match axes in the tool rail.

How to do it: Chapter 07 ↗

04 · Edge blending

Seams found for you.

Group the heads that overlap and press Auto-Blend. FieldLux measures where the beams meet on the surface and sets each edge in percent and metres. Pick Linear, Gamma 2.2 or S-Curve, mirror a feather to its partner and match brightness across the seam.

Measured lux stays pre-blend: the map shows each head at full white so the plan never hides a hot spot.

How to do it: Chapter 08 ↗

05 · Heatmap

Know the lux before the truck leaves.

Turn on the Heatmap overlay for Surface Lux, Pixel Density or Luminance. Read Surface Lux on an Absolute, Norm or Deviation scale, press Values for the 13pt Diagnostic with a uniformity grade, probe any point under the cursor, and set the venue's ambient light to read the contrast ratio.

FieldLux gives planning estimates, not calibrated field measurements. The Heatmap panel labels the basis of every map, and every head in today's library reads Physics Model.

How to do it: Chapters 09 to 11 ↗

06 · Play Dock

Put the content on the wall.

Output Setup finds the screens from where your beams land. Drop images or video from the Media Library, build a playlist, and corner-pin, mesh-warp, mask and soft-edge each head in Warp & Blend, and see the result live on the 3D model. For an immersive room, Floor-centred runs one video off the floor and up all four walls, with the projectors that share a face blended.

Play Dock previews the show. It does not output to hardware.

How to do it: Chapters 12, 13 and 20 ↗

07 · Lighting and fog

The rest of the rig, in the same scene.

Add moving heads, PARs, profiles, Fresnels, floods or strobes, import GDTF, MVR, IES and LDT files, and read an isolux working plane at any height. Add a Fog Machine and up to four projector beams and four fixture beams glow in the air.

Fixtures provide visual context and do not affect the projector lux calculation.

How to do it: Chapters 14 and 15 ↗

08 · Share and handoff

One link for the client, one PDF for the crew.

Create a Cloud Share link with a QR code, optional password and expiry, choose what viewers can do, and revoke it from Share Management. Export a PDF Tech Rider in one click, or an editable .flx.json project file. Projects are cloud-saved with autosave and version history.

How to do it: Chapters 16 to 18 ↗
